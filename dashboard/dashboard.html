Why it scored 0 laps: the EKF started 127° off

yaw 0° vs 127° → wall at t+2.5 s

imu_gnss_poser only applied the raceline heading inside its one-shot /set_initial_pose service. When AWSIM boots slowly — which is exactly what the other karts on the grid cause — autostart_orchestrator calls that service before the first GNSS sample, it fails with no GNSS data received yet, and the orchestrator proceeds anyway. The EKF then seeds from the GNSS/IMU orientation, which carries no usable yaw, so it starts at yaw 0° while the kart points 127°. The MPC sees the full heading error, ramps steering to full lock at the 0.6 rad/s rate limit, and drives off the grid into the wall 2.5 s after the start — beached at 0.004 m/s for the remaining 460 s with wall-recovery off. No tune can fix this; it is a localisation race condition, and it is the most likely reason a submission scored 0 laps.

Fix: seed the heading from the raceline on the first GNSS sample

wall penalty 474 s → 0 s

gnss_callback now applies the raceline yaw to initial_pose3d itself (via the try_apply_raceline_yaw helper, which existed but was never called), so the heading no longer depends on when — or whether — the orchestrator's service call lands. The EKF measurement topic keeps the GNSS/IMU orientation, unchanged. Re-run of the same scenario: the service call failed again as before, Publishing initial_pose3d (yaw from raceline) fired 11 s later, EKF started at 127°, and the kart pulled off the grid cleanly. The fix lives in aichallenge_submit/imu_gnss_poser, so it ships in the submission — unlike the orchestrator, which the organizers supply themselves.

A contact no longer ends the session: reverse out and rejoin

0 laps → 6/6 finished (R55 → R58)

AWSIM's own wall recovery is off in the official evaluation, so one contact used to beach the kart for the rest of the session — measured, that is 460 s at 0.004 m/s. The new stuck_recovery package sits last in the command chain (after collision_guard, which would otherwise brake for the very obstacle being escaped) and takes over when the car stops making progress while the controller still wants to drive: shift to reverse, back out ~3 m with the steering mirrored — yaw rate is v·tan(δ)/L, so the same angle turns the car the other way at v<0 — then shift up, aim at the raceline and hand control back. It arms only after the car has driven at 2 m/s once, so it cannot fire on the grid. Re-running the exact scenario that scored 0 laps in R55: finished=true, lap_count=6, position 1, 9 recoveries of ~5 s each, zero collision or wall penalties.

Still open: getting past a kart that is barely moving

49.6 s/lap in traffic vs 37.1 s clear

Recovery keeps the session alive but does not overtake. AWSIM's NPC karts lap in ~185 s (6.7 km/h) and are not published on /v2x/vehicle_positions, so collision_guard never sees them (zero EMERGENCY, zero slow: cap across all NPC runs) and cannot even slow for them, while lateral avoidance is off. The kart therefore drives into the blocker, digs itself out, and drives into it again — all 9 recoveries in R58 fired inside the same 5 m patch. Real opponents are separate vehicles that do appear over V2X and run at a competitive pace, so treat this as a bound on overtaking, not on completion.

What the kart is actually limited by

measured, 2026-07-27

The binding constraint is acceleration, and it is a power limit. Commanding a_max 3.0 m/s² yields only ≈0.4 m/s² at racing speed while braking reaches -4.1 m/s², and achieved a·v is roughly constant across 6–11 m/s. Raising a_max past ~3 does nothing. Because re-accelerating is so slow, every km/h carried through a corner is worth far more than any braking gain — which is why a high ay_max plus late braking, not a bigger a_max, is what took the lap from 45.8 s to 39.5 s.

Earlier versions of this page asserted a 30 km/h / 1.0 m/s² competition rule and a 43 s "physics floor". Neither is documented anywhere in this repo and both are contradicted by measurement, so they have been removed — confirm the real limits against the official rules before submitting.
